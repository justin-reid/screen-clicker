SCREEN CLICKER
What this does

Watch for a picture.
Tap where you choose.

You point a rule at a piece of the screen. When that picture shows up, the app taps the spot you picked — again and again, until you stop it.

  • Works in any app, no root, no ADB.
  • Everything runs on the phone. Nothing is uploaded.
Before anything works

Two permissions

Both start off. Nothing taps or draws over apps until you switch them on.

  • 1Accessibility service — lets it see the screen and tap for you.
  • 2Display over other apps — lets the rule editor and the floating panel sit on top of your game.
Enable accessibility Allow over apps
These buttons open the exact Android screen — you never have to hunt through Settings.
Once, at the first run

Share entire screen — not one app

Android asks what to share the first time a script runs. Pick Entire screen.

A “single app” grant looks like it works, but the script can only see that one window — so the panel tells you and the run stops. Just confirm again with Entire screen.

Shown by Android, not by us — but it is the step people get wrong, so the tutorial says it up front.
Step 1 of 3

Make a script

A script is one app’s worth of rules. New script, name it, and you are in.

  • Run only in this app (optional): paste the package name so the script stays quiet when that app is not in front.
  • Rules run in order, each with its own delay — that is what stops it from hammering the screen.
Not sure of the package name? Leave it blank while testing; the script then runs in whatever app is on screen.
Step 2 of 3 · the important one

Teach it one rule

  • 1Search area — box the part of the screen worth watching.
  • 2Template area — box the exact picture to look for, then Capture.
  • 3Click area — box where the tap should land.
  • 4Check — one test match, no tapping. Then Save.
Switch app leaves the toolbar on screen while you bring your game forward. Drag the title bar to move it out of the way.
Step 3 of 3

Run it, then get out of the way

Start it from the app, the Quick Settings tile, or the floating panel — the little ▶ bubble that sits over your game.

  • While a script runs the panel has no ✕. It is the stop button, so it stays put; minimize it to the bubble instead (it turns ■).
  • Options ▸ in the panel holds Highlight matches, Show taps and the runner log.
If nothing happens

Read the runner log

Options ▸ Runner log in the panel prints what the runner saw: the frame it captured, the region it searched, the best match score, and your threshold.

  • Score just under the threshold → redraw the template tighter, or lower the threshold in the rule.
  • Nothing at all found → Highlight matches draws the search area, so you can see whether the box is even in the right place.
